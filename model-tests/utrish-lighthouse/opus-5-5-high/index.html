About this miniature

The place

The Utrish lighthouse stands on Utrish Island off the cape of Bolshoy Utrish, about 20 km south-east of Anapa on the Black Sea coast of Russia, at the edge of the Utrish nature reserve. The pale brick tower with its sculpted band of faces was built in 1975 to a design by Anapa architect Boris Kamaev as a memorial to the Black Sea sailors of the Second World War. Beside it, the small 1911 lighthouse serves as the Chapel of St. Nicholas, patron saint of sailors. It is shown here as it stood before its relocation began in 2025.

This is an artistic miniature interpretation, not an exact survey. Distances are compressed, and shapes, colours and details are approximations made from photographs and a satellite view. The pier with its boats sits by the “Fort Utrish” restaurant on the cape’s north shore, beyond the village houses; the restaurant building itself is a simplified stand-in. The kiteboarder in the island’s lee is an added scene of life; the kite wears a CORE XR (2026 “XR X”, 9 m) style colourway modelled on the maker’s renders. All geometry and textures are generated in code.

THE PLACE
Utrish Island is a small, steep, rocky island separated from the Bolshoy Utrish cape by a strait only about 20 m wide. A narrow metal bridge crosses the strait, and a footpath runs along a low causeway from the bridge to the lighthouses. The island's shores are mostly sheer rock, so nobody can walk around it at the water's edge. Geologically the cape and island are the tongue of a huge ancient landslide ("Utrish" is usually translated as "landslide" or "collapsed mountain"), so the rock should read as broken, tilted, fractured strata of light grey-ochre limestone and marl, with fallen blocks at the waterline.

On the island stand two lighthouses:

A sculpted relief band runs around the tower. Nearby is a granite slab with memorial names. Show it as a slender pale tower with the relief band, a gallery and a lantern on top.
- The low 1911 lighthouse a short distance from the shoreline, converted into the small Chapel of St. Nicholas, patron saint of sailors, with a small dome. Show it in its pre-2025 state, before the chapel's relocation began.

They are covered in the reserve's relict, Mediterranean-looking open woodland: dark-green juniper, pistachio and Pitsunda pine, with pale rock showing through and dry summer grass.
